**Interpretation.** The dome did not slow the drive. The top speed and slope are the same within
the run-to-run scatter of the baseline (±4 %; the two baseline runs differed by 8.5 % at
+100 %), and the drive became more symmetric. At steady state the motor speed is not load-limited
here. The added inertia shows in the response: the step response now takes about twice as long
(median 1.27 s against 0.63 s to reach 90 % of the step speed, up to 2 s at 60 to 70 %), and
stalled low steps coast further before stopping. The low end is slightly worse. p0 moved up by 1
to 3 points, the operating-settings run needed 32 % instead of 25 to 27 %, and the velocity at the
raw minimum percent fell to 0.19 to 0.41 rad/s, which is stick-slip, not a usable steady speed.
The baseline expected a higher `min_percent`, a lower top speed and a smaller `k`. Only the first
held, and only modestly. The clear change is the sensor: position spikes at fixed angles that did
not exist without the dome.

## Observations

1. **Position spikes at fixed angles (new with the dome, `suggested_pass` false).** See "Sensor
   sweep" for the angles and counts. They need the mechanical check of plan phase 6 and a sensor
   sweep re-run after it. Pattern B (~200 deg reading ~13 deg) is close to 180 deg away from the
   true angle. Both the bench node and the plugin unwrap by shortest distance, so one B spike
   lasting one report shifts the unwrapped position by -360 deg for good. The telemetry
   `unwrapped_rad` went from -1609 deg to -1954 deg instead of about -1594 deg in step 17 of the
   operating run. The firmware's own `DomePosition` accumulates the same way (plan 2.1). Until
   the sensor is fixed, expect the continuous joint position to lose or gain whole turns and the
   velocity estimate to spike, which also affects the dome position controller. The speed fit of
   the raw run is unaffected: it contains a single A spike, which nets to zero.
2. **MaxSpeed** still has no effect at the top (operating / raw 0.99 to 1.01 at 80 to 100 %). See
   the section on the operating run for the low end, where the run cannot tell a small MaxSpeed
   effect from scatter. Configure `min_percent` from the operating run (32).
3. **Low end.** No step below 30 % ran continuously, in either run. ±30 % moved in the raw run
   (0.43 / 0.81 rad/s, from 170 deg and 243 deg) and stalled in the operating run (from 160 deg
   and 7 deg, stopping at 7 deg and 188 deg). In the sensor sweep, ±35 % ran for
   30 s at 1.46 / 1.05 rad/s. The slowest dependable speed with the dome is therefore about 1
   rad/s, at 32 to 35 %.
4. **The phase 7 acceptance point of 0.5 rad/s cannot be met with the dome.** The fit maps
   +0.5 rad/s to 25.9 % and -0.5 rad/s to 26.6 %. Both are below `min_percent` and clamp up to
   it: 27 % gives 0.19 / 0.41 rad/s with stick-slip (raw), and at the operating settings 30 %
   stalls and 32 % gives 0.74 to 1.0 rad/s. A 10 % tolerance around 0.5 rad/s is out of reach in
   both directions. The acceptance point needs a decision by the human, for example 1.0 rad/s
   (31.8 % and 32.4 % from the fit, just at the operating minimum) or 1.5 rad/s.
5. **Response time** roughly doubled (see the comparison). With Scaling off, this is the Syren
   ramp and current limit acting on the added inertia. Any controller gain tuning should use the
   with-dome response.
6. **Sign convention** unchanged: a positive percent moves the reported angle down, and
   `positive_velocity.percent_sign` is -1.
7. **Startup.** As on the baseline, `#DPREPORT28` timed out once and was retried, and
   `/diagnostics` showed `position_reporting_enabled: false` all session while reports flowed
   normally.
8. **Process note.** After the sensor sweep the idle bench node was stopped with
   `kill -INT $(pgrep -f ...)` from a shell whose command line contained the pattern, against the
   session rule. Only the idle bench node exited (it had already restored settings and written
   results); the controller manager, `robot_state_publisher` and the router were unaffected. The
   later stops used PIDs looked up in a separate call.
